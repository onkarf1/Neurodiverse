# StriveTrack: 3-minute pitch and demo script

Sign in as **Maya Brooks** before you start. Keep the app on **Alignment check** with **Practice a café opening routine** selected.

## 0:00 – The problem (30 seconds)

> Meet Ms. Patel, a special-education teacher with 14 students. Jordan is 16 and autistic. His IEP goal is to *plan* a work routine: choose the order of the steps himself. But the café worksheet Ms. Patel was handed says "Follow these steps in order" and numbers them. Jordan can finish it perfectly and never practice planning at all. Teachers catch this only if they have time, and they rarely do.

## 0:30 – The check (45 seconds)

Run the alignment check.

> StriveTrack compares the activity with Jordan's goal. In about ten seconds the AI builds an executive-function demand map: the goal targets planning and sequencing, and this worksheet does both *for* him. Every finding quotes the worksheet, and StriveTrack checks that each quote really appears in the text. If the AI invents a quote, the finding is thrown out and we say so.

## 1:15 – The fix (45 seconds)

Create a revision draft.

> The AI rewrites the activity at a grade-3 reading level, with picture-cue cards that are not numbered, so Jordan decides the order. It cannot drop his accommodations: code adds them word for word, and approval is blocked if any are missing.

Delete "Extra processing time" from the draft, save, and show the block. Restore it and approve.

## 2:00 – The session (40 seconds)

Choose **Run a practice session**. Turn on **Easier reading** and start **Thinking time**.

> Students never log in. Ms. Patel shows the cards, starts the thinking-time countdown, a support from his IEP, and records the order Jordan chose and *why*. That is evidence of planning she can bring to his IEP meeting. StriveTrack never scores mastery; the teacher decides.

## 2:40 – Why it matters and what's next (20 seconds)

Open **Activity history**.

> Every check, edit, approval, and session is on the record. AI suggests, StriveTrack verifies, the teacher decides. Next: a one-semester teacher pilot, school sign-on, FERPA-ready hosting, and importing goals from IEP systems.

## Likely judge questions

- **Is student data sent to the AI?** No. Only the goal and activity text. Learner codes, sessions, and notes stay local.
- **What if the AI is wrong or offline?** Quotes are verified, supports are enforced in code, and local rules take over if the model does not respond.
- **Why teachers only?** The records are about the learner. Teachers and case managers own them, as they do IEP paperwork.
- **How would it scale?** See the roadmap in `README.md`: pilot, real sign-in, privacy agreements, IEP integration.
